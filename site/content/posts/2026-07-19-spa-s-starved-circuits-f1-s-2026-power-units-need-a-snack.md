Spa-Francorchamps, the beloved roller coaster of Ardennes, has once again thrown a wrench into F1's grand plans. According to insiders, the 2026 power units—supposedly the bees knees of hybrid efficiency—are struggling to keep their batteries juiced on the legendary circuit.

Imagine a hungry teenager after a growth spurt: that's the 2026 PU at Spa. The long straights and high-speed corners, while thrilling for fans, are a nightmare for energy recovery. Turns out, the combination of heavy braking zones and limited time to recharge makes Spa a 'energy-starved circuit,' as the tech wizards delicately put it.

F1's engineers are now scratching their heads, wondering how to cram more energy harvesting into a lap that's already as packed as a Belgian chocolate shop. Perhaps they should consider adding a few hairpins? Or maybe just let the drivers pedal harder? Either way, the 2026 regulations might need a rethink before Spa becomes a battery-draining black hole.

But let's be real: if anyone can figure this out, it's F1. If not, we might see drivers pushing their cars down the Kemmel straight—now that's the fan engagement Liberty Media never knew it needed.
